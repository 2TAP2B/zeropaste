# accounts and administration

Accounts are optional: anonymous pasting is the default and needs no cookie, no session, no tracking. But a passkey unlocks a private dashboard - and on locked instances it is the door.

## passkeys

- Registration and sign-in run over **WebAuthn** (the one third-party Go module in the codebase).
- Sessions are HMAC-signed cookies (`zp.session`), HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax, 30 days.
- Passkeys need a **domain**: IP-literal hosts (`http://192.168.1.10:8080`) cannot hold them - open the site over its hostname. The browser pane tells you exactly that.
- Sign-ins are discoverable: pick your passkey, no username needed.

## the dashboard

`/dash` (sign in via the header's account button) shows:

- **shares** - every active paste you created, with kind, views, expiry/burn and copy/delete;
- **reverse** - your drop-link inboxes and arrivals ([see reverse shares](/guide/reverse-shares));
- **account** - display name and passkey management;
- **config** - the admin surface (below).

## admin rights

- The **first registered account owns the instance**: it gets the admin flag and the config tab.
- The admin promotes friends in the config card's ACCOUNTS section - one switch per identity. Demoting the **last** admin is refused, so the instance never loses its door.
- Owner transfer = promote a friend, then demote yourself.
- Existing instances that predate the flag migrate at boot: with no admin found, the **oldest identity** is promoted (the boot log names it); `ADMIN_ID=<identity id>` picks explicitly instead.

:::note
The real emergency hatch is the same as always: anyone with access to the server can edit `data/identities/<id>.json` directly. That is root anyway.
:::

## invites

When registration is closed or the instance is locked, invite links let a friend in anyway:

1. In the config card's INVITES section, mint a link (single- or multi-use).
2. The friend opens `https://your.site/#invite=<token>` once - the token sticks in their tab.
3. They register their passkey; the link dies after its last use or when you revoke it.

Invite tokens are validated at the start and the end of the ceremony; abandoned ceremonies never burn a use.

## locking the instance down

The config card's **who can paste** choice (or the env vars on first boot) gives three exclusive postures:

| mode | compose | reads | who registers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **anyone** | open | open | per the accounts choice |
| **gate passphrase** | unlock screen first | open | per the accounts choice |
| **members only** | passkey sign-in required | open | per the accounts choice |

Reads **never** lock: a share link decrypts for anyone who has it, with or without an account. Burn-after-reading deletes still work for anonymous readers - the zero-knowledge contract does not depend on accounts.
